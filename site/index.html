Privacy, in plain words

What it reads, and when.

To show your usage, ClaudeBar needs your claude.ai login. Here is every step, so you can say no.

Only on your clickClaudeBar does nothing with your browser until you press Connect. No click, no read.
Your loginOn Connect it looks for your claude.ai session cookie in Claude desktop, Chrome, Brave, Edge, Arc or Safari. macOS may ask for your password to unlock the browser's Safe Storage key.
Staying connectedAfter you connect that way, ClaudeBar re-reads the login every few hours and whenever claude.ai signs you out, so it keeps working. If you paste your key yourself, it never reads a browser.
Where it livesOne item in your own Keychain. Disconnect deletes it.
Who it talks toOnly claude.ai, to ask for your usage. No analytics, no accounts, no telemetry.
Prefer not to?Paste your session key yourself instead. It works the same.
What can breakThese are the same pages claude.ai uses, not a public API. If Claude changes them, ClaudeBar pauses until it is updated.
Read it yourselfOpen source under MIT. Every line is on GitHub.
Install

Three steps. One is a bit fiddly.

1
Download

Grab ClaudeBar.dmg from the latest release. It needs macOS 14 or later.

Download ClaudeBar.dmg
2
Drag to Applications

Open the disk image and drop ClaudeBar onto the Applications folder.

3
Open it once, the careful way

ClaudeBar is not signed with an Apple developer certificate, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open System Settings, go to Privacy and Security, scroll down to the message about ClaudeBar and click Open Anyway.

Or paste this in Terminal once:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/ClaudeBar.app
Why unsigned?

Apple charges developers a yearly fee to sign apps. ClaudeBar is free and open source, so version 1 ships unsigned. You can read every line on GitHub, or build it yourself.
